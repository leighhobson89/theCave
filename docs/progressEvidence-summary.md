# Progress Evidence — Full Content Summary (spoilers)

Every entry in `assets/progressEvidence.json`, listed in `progressEvidenceId` order.
For each: what the record/page/fax actually says when opened, and whether it
awards a piece of Evidence (Photos/Reports). Any name, code, or password

## ZoomSearch (00001–00008)

### 00001 — Black Pine Mine Exhausted
*itemId: `silvermineentrance`*
An old 1851 poster reprint from the Saskatchewan Mining Gazette: the Black Pine
silver mine was declared "Exhausted" on 1 July 1851 after two months without a
new seam; work was moving to a new shaft. The photo is hand-labelled **"No
More" — 30/06/1851**, and shows a mine cart stencilled *Whitmore & Sons
Foundry*.
**Evidence granted:** ✅ Photo — *"No More!"*

### 00002 — John Baxley
*itemId: `johnbaxley`*
Profile of John Baxley, a former Police diver/photographer who documented the
Arnie Spencer case scene and later joined the Forestry Imaging Enthusiasts
club (1928). Includes a rare 1901 photo of him with the rest of the Mounties
search party outside Black Pine Shaft B.
**🔑 Clue:** the photo is captioned in cursive **"James, Emile, Thomas, John,
William, George"** — naming the six-man search party: (James) Fletcher,
(Emile) Beaulieu, (Thomas) O'Rourke, (John) Baxley, (William) McLeod, (George)
Mackenzie.
**Evidence granted:** ✅ Photo — *"Search Party outside Black Pine Silver Mine
Shaft B"*

### 00003 — We put the Wackiness in Gold Mining!
*itemId: `minecart`*
A joke page from "Wacky Miners" — they don't actually sell anything, and just
redirect you to the real supplier.
**🔑 Clue:** gives the URL `http://www.whitmore-sons-iron-machinery-co.com/mining`
**Evidence granted:** ❌ None

### 00004 — Apples for All!
*itemId: `margaretmcleod`*
Bio of Margaret McLeod, William McLeod's wife — ran a small apple orchard near
Maple Grove, Ontario after William's 1920 retirement, and continued it after
his 1957 death.
**Evidence granted:** ❌ None

### 00005 — Local Author is heir to the Whitmore Empire
*itemId: `henrywhitmore`*
Bio of Henry "Harry" Whitmore, Arthur Whitmore's eldest son. Gave up his
inheritance of Whitmore & Sons to his brother Jerome in 1912 to become an
author/naturalist and caving enthusiast instead. Died c. 1965.
**Evidence granted:** ❌ None

### 00006 — Unexpectedly gains control of Whitmore & Sons Iron
*itemId: `jeromewhitmore`*
Jerome Whitmore unexpectedly inherits the family firm after his brother Henry
chooses to write books instead.
**Evidence granted:** ❌ None

### 00007 — Brian Whitmore keeps the family business alive!
*itemId: `brianwhitmore`*
Brian succeeds his father Jerome as company president in 1963, keeping
Jerome's branch of the family in control of the business.
**Evidence granted:** ❌ None

### 00008 — J & T Fairchild Valuation Experts & Insurance Brokers
*itemId: `fairchilds`*
Advert for Joseph & Therese Fairchild's valuation/insurance business.
**🔑 Clue:** gives a demo record code, **`05-33-22-02-03`**, to look up on the
Police Database, and a link to their decode book:
`http://fairchilds-valuations-and-insurance.com/insRecordsCodes`. Also notes
"Route 07 = Fairchilds" as a running joke about their own reference number.
**Evidence granted:** ✅ Report — *"Insurance Record Template"*

## Library Archive (10001–10003)

### 10001 — Mysteries of the Old North West
*itemId: `mysteryoldnw`*
Preface of Hannah Fletcher's 1906 book. She interviewed search-party
witnesses; her own husband (a Constable) recalled finding four sets of
footprints (two adult) in the cave, despite witness accounts that only two
boys went in alone — a mystery.
**🔑 Clue:** the photographed title page has a handwritten pencil note in the
top-right corner reading **"j.fletcher / oscar123"** — this is literally the
login for Constable James Fletcher's Police Records account (privilege level
1).
**Evidence granted:** ✅ Photo — *"Preface of Mysteries of the Old North West"*

### 10002 — Guardians Of The North
*itemId: `guardiansofthenorth`*
1988 retrospective by Cat J. Roman on honourable NWMP service members.
**🔑 Clue:** the photographed page has a handwritten pencil note in the
top-left margin reading **"Ontario apples (t.mcleod / apple1)"** — the login
for the Canada Newspaper Archive's paid "Subscriber" account.
**Evidence granted:** ✅ Photo — *"Guardians of the North"*

### 10003 — Strange Things Found In Even Stranger Places
*itemId: `strangethingsfoundinevenstrangerplaces`*
1907 book by Henry "Harry" Whitmore — fifty stories of objects found where
they shouldn't be. Page 83 tells "Lieutenant William McLeod and the Golden
Pendant": during the 1901 search, McLeod found a small pendant on a ledge in
flooded Shaft B, "engraved with a name."
**🔑 Clue (a dead end, deliberately):** the library's copy is vandalised — the
engraved name is scribbled out in thick pen so it can't be read from this
copy. A note on the record asks anyone who knows who damaged the book to
inform the librarian.
**Evidence granted:** ✅ Photo — *"Strange Things Found In Even Stranger
Places"*

## Police Records (20001–20009)

### 20001 — Constable James Fletcher
*itemId: `jamesfletcher`*
Constable James Fletcher, awarded the King's Police Medal for his part in the
1901 Black Pine search; retired 1934; lived in Gray Stacks, Alberta with wife
Hannah (author of *Mysteries of the Old North West*, see 10001) and pet
Labrador **Oscar**; died 1951. (No login needed — public record.)
**🔑 Clue:** the dog's name, Oscar, is the origin of the "oscar123" password
found written in 10001.
**Evidence granted:** ❌ None

### 20002 — Corporal Emile Beaulieu
*itemId: `emilebeaulieu`*
Corporal Emile Beaulieu, retired 1915 to become a professional climber;
posthumously featured on the cover of *Rockface Monthly* (March 1972) after
the magazine interviewed her daughter. Died 1955. (No login needed.)
**Evidence granted:** ❌ None

### 20003 — Lieutenant William McLeod
*itemId: `williammcleod`*
Lt. William McLeod's NWMP record; retired 1920 to rural Ontario with wife
Margaret; died 1957. Illustrated with an excerpt from *Guardians of the North*
showing his retirement Certificate of Honour presentation, June 1920. (Needs
privilege level 2 — `b.whitmore`.)
**Evidence granted:** ❌ None

### 20004 — Corporal George Mackenzie
*itemId: `georgemackenzie`*
Corporal George Mackenzie served until 1931, died 1954; locally famous for
reputedly growing the longest moustache in Canada, shown having it measured
aged 80 in 1952. (No login needed.)
**Evidence granted:** ❌ None

### 20005 — Professional Cave Diver and Corporal
*itemId: `thomasorourke`*
Thomas O'Rourke's record — avid caver, part of the 1901 search party, founded
the Honey Dew Caving Club (Sept 1901), which later mapped the Black Pine
system; died 1911 abseiling in France; awarded a posthumous Medal of Honor.
**🔑 Clue:** gives the caving club's URL, `http://honeydewcavingclub.com`.
(Needs privilege level 1.)
**Evidence granted:** ❌ None

### 20006 — The Iron magnate who chose Policing first!
*itemId: `arthurwhitmore`*
Sergeant Arthur Whitmore's record — a Whitmore family member who chose
policing over the family iron business; led the 1901 search party; an
enthusiastic amateur photographer, believed to have taken the famous
search-party photo himself (explaining his own absence from it, letting
police photographer John Baxley appear in his place). (Needs privilege level
2 — opening this record triggers a second, Level 3 credentials fax, see
50004.)
**Evidence granted:** ❌ None

### 20007 — GOLD PENDANT
*itemId: `goldenpendant`*
Evidence log for the gold pendant Lt. William McLeod found at the bottom of
flooded Shaft B on 15 Nov 1901, engraved on the reverse with **"P. A.
WORTHING"** and attached to a broken gold chain. A November 1901 follow-up
note says the engraving was matched to a J & T Fairchild insurance policy held
by local resident Anthony Worthing, who was questioned and released without
charge — case still officially open. (Needs privilege level 3 —
`t.fairchild`.)
**Evidence granted:** ✅ Photo — *"GOLD PENDANT"* (shows the P. A. WORTHING
engraving)

### 20008 — Fairchild insurance record 05-33-22-02-03
*itemId: `fairchildinsurancerecordexampletemplate`*
The full Fairchild valuation record for a bronze garden statue ("The Water
Bearer") owned by John Hollingworth, dated 14 March 1901 — this is the demo
record referenced by the Fairchild advert (00008), useful as an example of
what a decoded record looks like. (No login needed.)
**Evidence granted:** ❌ None

### 20009 — Fairchild insurance record 09-24-49-02-07
*itemId: `fairchildinsurancerecordworthingpendant`*
Fairchild insurance record for a "gold cross pendant… engraved P. A.
WORTHING," insured by Percival Anthony Worthing in 1893 and transferred by a
1897 endorsement to his son, **Anthony Worthing**, on the original holder's
death — directly ties the pendant found in the mine (20007) to the Worthing
family. (Needs privilege level 3.)
**Evidence granted:** ❌ None

## Standalone Pages (40001–40003)

### 40001 — Buy your Mining Machinery from Whitmore & Sons
*itemId: `whitmoresonsironmachineryco`*
Whitmore & Sons company page with a welcome message from Brian Whitmore;
traces the family's mining-machinery pedigree back to 1840s Black Pine.
Jokes that only Grandpa Arthur (went into policing) and Uncle Harry (became
an author) didn't stay in the family trade.
**Evidence granted:** ❌ None

### 40002 — An Important and Historic Club
*itemId: `honeydewcavingclub`*
History of the Honey Dew Caving Club, founded September 1901 by Thomas
O'Rourke, which mapped much of the Black Pine cave system before folding
after O'Rourke's fatal 1911 accident; their collective map was released in
1912.
**🔑 Clue:** the team photo is captioned with all eight members' names — Thomas
O'Rourke, Simon Cresswell, Henry Whitmore, Robert Johnstone, *Unknown*,
**Anthony Worthing**, Sam Henderson, Paul Greenwood. The second image is the
hand-drawn 1912 "Black Pine Cave System" map itself, marking Shaft A, "Askew
Cave," and several unexplored pockets.
**Evidence granted:** ✅ Two photos — the club team photo and the 1912 cave
system map

### 40003 — Decode Book for Insurance Records
*itemId: `fairchildsinsurancerecordscodes`*
The full Fairchild insurance-code cipher key: every code is five numbers long,
read in order Colour → Object → Name → Document → Route, with every numbered
index listed out in full. This is the tool needed to decode any Fairchild
record code found elsewhere (e.g. `05-33-22-02-03` in 20008, or
`09-24-49-02-07` in 20009).
**Evidence granted:** ✅ Report — *"Insurance Decode Book"*

## Noticeboard timeline — one representative item per date

Working list for the noticeboard's empty date frames. Every event in
`story-timeline.md` §1, in date order. For each, **one single item** —
usually the specific photo, sometimes just the specific line of text where
no photo exists — chosen as the single best representation of *that exact
dated moment*, not just "the most important thing" about whoever is
involved. Where the underlying date itself is approximate/relative rather
than a fixed day, it's flagged **❓**. This is a first pass for you to swap
in actual photo cut-outs against later; where two items share a date they're
listed as (a)/(b) rather than reordered against each other.
