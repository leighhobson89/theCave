In Canada, there was a man called John and his wife, a woman called Diane. They moved far out of Toronto and in to the countryside to start a farming business, and search for a better quality of life. John had earned a comfortable living in banking, but the stress had begun to wear him down, so they decided to leave the city behind.  They bought a house with some of their money, and it came with a lot of land. They repaired the outside buildings. They got animals and gradually built up a successful farm. They sold milk, cheese, beef, and wool, and before long the farm was thriving. 

Over time they grew their family and eventually they had two boys, Andrew and Arnie. The boys grew up helping on the farm and exploring the surrounding countryside in their free time as typical children like to do. They found a large, deep forest with paths running through it and enjoyed exploring there. They attended the local school, the farm was doing fine, really speaking this new life had turned out pretty well.

John and Diane decided to employ two farm hands to help out. They were responsible for feeding and milking the animals, collecting the hay, tending the crops and carrying out other farm duties when required. Both received a meagre wage, along with board and lodging in the barn. They were named Simon and Tony. One could suppose they were each in their early thirties.

One day, during the school holidays in July 1901, the boys told their parents they were going out exploring, just as they always did. By this time, Andrew was twelve and Arnie was nine. They packed a lunch and set off for the forest, following the route they'd known for years.  They were enjoying walking along, three or four hours in to the forest, when suddenly a large scary looking animal emerged from the trees and started snarling at them. Frightened, the boys ran without looking back, off the path and into the trees down a narrow trail.

They were running, weaving between trees and bushes. They just kept running for a good minute and a half.  When they felt they had put some distance them and where it had come out, and they could no longer hear it, they slowed down and looked back, listening carefully. The forest had become quiet again. Whatever it was, it hadn't followed them. They caught their breath, and looking around, they realized they were no longer on the main path.

It was then that Arnie noticed something unusual. Partly hidden behind thick and creeping vegetation, was what looked like the entrance to a cave. "Come on," he said to Andrew. "Let's go and have a look". The cave looked dark, and the plants growing around the entrance almost completely concealed it, making it difficult to spot unless you were standing nearby. The boys went inside. After they turned a corner, it quickly became much darker, though there was still a little reflected sunlight reaching inside. They could hear the occasional sound of water droplets and saw a few insects as they explored. The cave appeared to be fairly small, consisting of what looked like a single cavern rather than anything particularly grand. They decided to sit down and eat their packed lunch. 

Arnie finished eating before Andrew and began wandering around the cavern, throwing stones against the walls to listen to the echoes. He was shouting into the cave and listening as his voice echoed back. Then one of the stones flew over a pile of rocks. Expecting to hear it strike a wall or the ground, they were curious when they heard no sound at all. 

Arnie threw another stone and the same thing happened. It seemed very strange. 

The boys walked over to the pile of rocks. They could just make out the point where the sunlight reached the back of the cave. Leaning over the rocks, they looked down and saw nothing but pitch blackness. There was an enormous hole that disappeared into the darkness. Beyond the point where the sun's rays could reach, there was no way to see inside. Arnie said, "Wow, this is so exciting. We need to come back and explore this". They agreed that they would return the following day with lights, but they agreed that they mustn't tell their parents. "Whatever you do, don't say anything Because they definitely won't let us come back. If they find out the cave is there, they'll be even more against it. So, don't say a word." stated Andrew. Excited by their discovery, the boys left the cave, found their way back to the main forest path and began walking home. Partway home, they met a man walking in the opposite direction.

The man smiled warmly and stopped to speak to them. "You two look as though you've had quite an adventure," he said. "Where have you been?" "We found a cave," Arnie answered excitedly. "A cave?" The man's eyebrows lifted. "That's unusual. Whereabouts?" Before Andrew could answer, Arnie continued. "Well, we were walking along this path when a really big wolf came out and chased us." Andrew nodded. "At least, we thought it was a wolf. We ran as fast as we could and ended up on a path we'd never been on before. After a while we stopped, and it wasn't chasing us anymore. That's when we noticed the cave." Arnie pointed back into the forest. "You follow the main path for quite a long way. Then there's a smaller path. There's a big rock beside it, and the cave is hidden behind lots of plants. We had to pull them aside to get inside." The man's expression changed almost imperceptibly. "The one hidden behind the overgrown entrance?" The boys exchanged a glance. "Yes," Arnie replied. "That's the one." "I know the cave." He was silent for a moment before continuing. "It's a fascinating place. Very few people ever find it." He looked at the boys and said, "If you ever go back, be careful. There's a large unexplored hole at the back." He let the words hang for a moment, and then continued, "People say it's bottomless, no one has ever touched the bottom, but say they hear strange cries coming up from the bottom of the hole." Arnie grinned. "Really??  We already saw the hole." "You did?" The man smiled faintly. "Then you know why people tell stories about it." Andrew shifted uneasily. "You aren't thinking of going back, are you?" "Yes tomorrow morning!" Arnie exclaimed excitedly. "Well..." the man said. "If you do, be careful around that hole." He smiled politely and started walking away. After a few steps, he paused. "Funny... I haven't heard anyone mention that old mine in years." He looked back at them for just a moment and then without another word, he continued along the path, disappearing amongst the trees. The boys walked back home.

"You are a little late!" shouted their mother Diane as they arrived back, just as the sun was setting, but smiled, happy to see them. "Your food is almost ready!" "What have you been doing, where have you been?" The boys answered that they had spent the day exploring new paths through the forest. That evening, in their bedroom, the two brothers quietly made plans to return to the cave the following morning, and they went to sleep...

"Mother! Father! Help!" screamed Andrew as he stumbled into the farmyard. John and Diane rushed from the farmhouse, followed a few seconds later by Tony and Simon coming out of the barn. "What the hell!" grunted John. "Oh my God!" screamed Diane. They ran as fast as they could to where Andrew had collapsed in the yard. He was exhausted and trembling. 

His face was covered in deep scratches, his clothes were torn, blood ran down one leg from a long cut, and his left hand had been nearly severed. He could barely catch his breath. "Arnie..." he gasped. His parents stared at him in horror. "Arnie is gone!" "What happened? Where were you? Oh my God, Andrew, tell me now what happened! John, go and get a bandage and some clean water!" Everyone gathered around Andrew in the farmyard to listen to his account...

"Arnie woke me at five o'clock that morning. He couldn't sleep.  We found a cave yesterday and going back to it was all he could think about. 

"You found a WHAT?" Diane exclaimed. 

"A cave." Diane looked horrified.  Andrew continued.

"He kept shaking me, saying, 'Andrew, I can't sleep. I want to go and see it again. Come on, wake up!' We got dressed, and I prepared a packed lunch for both of us downstairs. Outside, we could hear the cows being milked in the shed. Before leaving, we headed down there where Simon was working. I asked him, 'Where's Tony?' 'About somewhere,' Simon replied. We quietly collected a little fresh milk, said goodbye, and headed to the tool shed. I tried the handle and said, 'I thought this was always locked.' Arnie whispered, 'So did I. They must have forgotten to lock it last night.' We opened the door and went inside. We bundled some straw tightly together, wrapped it in our handkerchiefs, and made two torches. Then we found some kerosene, soaked the torches, and picked up two small pieces of flint. I said, 'We can light them with these flints against the rocks.' With our milk, the torches, and our packed lunch, we had everything we needed." 

Andrew grunted in pain before continuing.

"At around seven o'clock, we pretended we were only just getting up and told Father we were going for another day exploring the forest. He reminded us that we should help around the farm first, but we begged him. 'We have all the holidays to help. Please let us go!' Eventually, you agreed," Andrew said, looking at John. "Then Arnie asked where Mother was, and you said, 'She's still asleep. Don't disturb her.' We said 'Okay. Anyway, we're going!' You smiled and said, 'Have fun, and be careful. Don't go too far.' Arnie and I looked at each other, shouted, 'Bye, Father!' and walked outside, letting the door slam shut behind us."

"The cave is roughly two or three miles into the forest," Andrew continued, "so after about two hours we reached the forest path where we'd seen that scary animal the day before, it was like a wolf thing. At the large rock we turned onto the smaller trail. There was no sign of it this time, and so we carried on until we reached the cave." 

"What were you thinking?" Diane cried. "Wolves? Caves? You'll never set foot in that forest again!"  Diane was distraught, Andrew still remained calm and was trying to spit out the tale, although clearly in a lot of pain, and blood was starting to seep through the bandage on his hand.

"The entrance was easy to find now that the plants had already been pushed aside. The sun was shining straight in to the cave as it was early still, so its light reached much deeper inside than it had the day before. Everything was much easier to see. That's when we noticed loads of spiders and insects covering parts of the walls, and bats hanging above us.

It scared us a bit. I was braver than Arnie though and I said, 'Nothing happened yesterday. We didn't disturb anything. Why would today be any different?' Arnie looked around and knew I was right. The cavern was large and open. The bats were high above us, and the spiders weren't anywhere near us. So we carried our torches over to the pile of rocks and laid them on top until we needed them. We climbed onto the rocks again to go and see the hole. Even though the cave was brighter than the day before, the sunlight still couldn't reach into the hole. The opening lay between the rocks and the back wall of the cave, hidden completely in the pitch black shadow of the rocks.

I said, 'We'll need a torch after all.' and we went about lighting one, and planning to save the other for later. I struck the flint. Sparks flew everywhere. After a few tries, one finally caught the straw of the torch. The kerosene ignited almost instantly, and the torch burst into a bright flame. It burned steadily, glowing red-orange with a small blue flame at its base. It looked as though it would last quite a while. Holding the torch, I leaned over the rocks while Arnie stood beside me."

Diane could not believe her ears, and John was surprised too, perhaps at how little they knew their son, or not believing how their sons aged just twelve and nine would go off and do all this without saying a word.

Andrew was looking horrified as he explained "The opening was roughly ten feet across. The back wall of the cave stood behind it, with the hole itself between us and the wall. The torchlight shone down a couple of feet but below this was just pitch black and we could not see the bottom...

Suddenly, Andrew sighed and slipped out of consciousness.

Unfortunately, Andrew succombed to his injury, after having told only half of the story, Arnie was never found. Whatever happened to them remains a mystery until now...
